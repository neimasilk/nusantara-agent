The nusantara-agent paper is a **portfolio paper**, not just an academic submission. The user explicitly framed it (2026-05-18) as demonstrating skill across three target fields simultaneously:

- **Formal Methods** — Answer Set Programming, Clingo, 95 expert-verified rules, non-monotonic reasoning, formal symbolic encoding of customary law.
- **NLP** — Multi-LLM benchmark, Indonesian legal text understanding, statistical evaluation (Wilson CI, McNemar, Fleiss kappa), prompt engineering, token economy.
- **Neuro-Symbolic** — The very question of when symbolic knowledge helps neural models. The Phase 2 finding that rules *hurt* accuracy is itself a neuro-symbolic contribution (negative result with identified mechanism).

**How to apply:**

- Frame the paper so each of the three fields can independently see why it should hire/cite the author.
- The negative result ("rules hurt") is a *strength* in this framing — it demonstrates evidence-following rigor, not a weakness. Don't bury it.
- When advising on venues, surface options that hit each field: Knowledge-Based Systems (neuro-symbolic Q1), ACL/EMNLP Findings (NLP), Journal of Logic and Computation / Journal of Automated Reasoning (formal methods). A specialised Legal NLP workshop is also a fit but rates lower for portfolio impact.
- Keep the ASP rules + the benchmark releasable as standalone open-source resources. Even if the paper integrates them, separate citable artefacts strengthen the portfolio.
- Prefer one well-rounded paper over splitting into multiple — three angles in one piece is rarer and more memorable than three separate small contributions.
