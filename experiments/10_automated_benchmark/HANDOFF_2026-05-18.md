## 3. What the negative result actually means (mechanism)

Confusion matrices in `analysis/phase1_summary.json` show the rules-induced errors concentrate on:

- **B → C drift** (adat-dominant cases reclassified as synthesis): the rules list both adat AND national norms side-by-side, which makes "C — synthesis" feel like the safe answer.
- **B → A drift** (adat-dominant cases reclassified as national-dominant): the rules name national statutes (KUHPerdata, UU 1/1974, UUPA), which makes national law salient.

Both directions push the model **away from confidently applying pure adat reasoning**. Phase 1's "hard cases" (10 cases that fail across all 9 LLM conditions) cluster heavily on B-labelled GS-XXXX cases — empirical evidence for a systematic LLM blind spot on pure customary reasoning.

This is the *neuro-symbolic mechanism finding*. It's not just "rules hurt", it's "rules hurt because they bias the model toward the wrong synthesis class". That mechanism is portable: any prompt that lists multiple legal frameworks together will likely produce the same effect on these models.

### Neuro-Symbolic angle
- The central question — when does symbolic knowledge help neural models? — gets a counter-intuitive answer
- Length-vs-content diagnostic isolates *content* as the bias driver
- Mechanism identified: B→C and B→A drift
- Replicates and extends F-011 from project history
- **Selling point:** "Negative result with mechanism. Adding expert-verified rules to LLM context degrades accuracy across three vendors; the bias survives a 50% length cut. This isn't a 'rules don't help' shrug — it's a specific identifiable error pattern."

The single paper can hit all three by structuring it as:
- Intro & Related Work (legal pluralism context)
